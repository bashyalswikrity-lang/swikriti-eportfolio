Roles from the Group Roles Report E3

Lift Right

A first-person 360° simulation that runs in a normal browser. A warehouse worker walks a virtual warehouse and is scored in real time on the safety decisions made during a lift.

Why it is needed E7

Problem with the current approachHow Lift Right responds
A one-off classroom induction or a static poster is quickly forgotten once a worker is back on the floor under time pressure.The simulation can be repeated in five to ten minutes, so refresher training is cheap enough to actually happen.
Paper sign-off sheets show that a worker attended a session, not that they can apply the technique.Each checkpoint needs an active, scored decision, so a pass reflects demonstrated judgement and not attendance.
Supervisors have no easy way to see who is competent, only who signed a form.The Admin Command Center shows per-worker logins, attempts, time on task and best score, exportable to CSV.

How a training run works

  1. Sign in. Create a worker account and read the pre-start briefing: W A S D to walk, mouse to look, E to lift.
  2. Walk the warehouse. Move between the Loading Dock, Warehouse Floor and Racking Aisle.
  3. Clear six checkpoints. Each has a knowledge check, and the HUD shows time, score, zone and the step list.
  4. Choose how to move the load. Manual lift, team lift or trolley, offered to suit the weight.
  5. See the score. The attempt, time and best score are saved to the worker’s record.

Weeks 1 to 2

Research and the PACT analysis

I started by reviewing 360° training tools and warehouse safety training, then drafted the PACT analysis (People, Activities, Context, Technology) for the warehouse use case. The finished design pack uses it to explain who Lift Right is for.

E7E10E5 · W1–2
PACT user groupWhat was foundDesign consequence
Warehouse operativesWide range of ages, fitness and gaming experience, including new starters on their first day.Controls limited to mouse-look and W-A-S-D, with a short pre-start briefing instead of a long tutorial.
Workers who use English as a second languageThe floor is expected to include them.Short, plain prompts paired with visual cues such as a highlighted load and marker colour.
Supervisors and training administratorsTime-poor and non-technical. They need a fast answer to who is trained.A single summarised Admin Command Center table with a one-click CSV export.
